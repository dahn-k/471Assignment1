Write Up

About Dataset

The Daily Weather in the U.S., 2017 dataset contains daily U.S. weather measurements from NOAA’s Daily Global Historical Climatology Network. We selected station names, states, minimum temperature (tmin), maximum temperature (tmax), longitude, latitude, and date to compare temperature extremes across stations, states, and seasons.

Design Decisions and Interactions

The primary goal for our visualization was to observe patterns or outliers in temperature extremes for stations over time. We chose a line chart with dates on the x-axis, temperature (tmin or tmax) on the y-axis, and various colors for each station to make changes over time easier to observe. We implemented dropdown filters for states, radio buttons to switch between minimum and maximum temperatures, and date and longitude sliders to narrow the displayed data. Tooltips show the station name and coordinates. Station search, highlighting, and dimming help users isolate individual trends. A color-coded legend and introductory annotation provide additional guidance. Alternatives included a scatter plot to explore relationships between variables, such as temperature and precipitation or elevation. However, variables like average temperature, snowfall, and precipitation contained many missing values, making them less suitable for our analysis. While examining data, we considered available variables, filtered states based on data availability, made sure each included state had at least one station, and removed states with many missing values. Because average temperature (tavg) was missing for over a third of the records, we chose to exclude it. We also verified that the date range supported interactive filtering.

Data Sources and References

The data used was the csv file from the U.S. weather measurements from 2017, provided by NOAA’s Daily Global Historical Climatology Network. We also used the Lab 4+5 dropdown example when implementing the dropdown interaction in our visualization.
